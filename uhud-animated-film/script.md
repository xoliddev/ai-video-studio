# Uhud — A Story of Patience and Obedience

Narration script (English). Total length: 5:52

## Title  (0:00)

In the third year after the Hijrah, beneath a mountain north of Madinah, a small band of believers would face one of their hardest tests.

This is the story of Uhud.

## Makkah  (0:18)

A year earlier, at Badr, the Quraysh of Makkah had suffered a defeat they could not forget.

Their leaders swore revenge, and poured the profits of an entire trade caravan into a new army.

## March  (0:33)

In the month of Shawwal, three thousand men marched north, with two hundred horses and seven hundred coats of armour.

Abu Sufyan led them. And on their right wing rode the cavalry of a brilliant young commander: Khalid ibn al-Walid.

## Madinah  (0:52)

In Madinah, the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, gathered his companions for counsel.

His own view was to defend the city from within its walls.

But many young men, who had missed the battle of Badr, longed to meet the enemy in the open field.

The Prophet accepted the counsel of the majority, and put on his armour.

## Split  (1:17)

About one thousand men set out.

But on the way, Abdullah ibn Ubayy turned back with nearly three hundred of his followers.

Now only seven hundred remained, against an army more than four times their size.

## Map  (1:35)

The Prophet placed his army with Mount Uhud at its back.

To one side rose a small hill. From there, enemy horsemen could sweep around behind the Muslim lines.

So he posted fifty archers on that hill, under the command of Abdullah ibn Jubayr.

## Archers  (1:56)

His command to them was clear.

Even if you see birds snatching at us, do not leave your place until I send for you.

And even if you see us defeat them, do not leave your place until I send for you.

## Battle  (2:14)

The battle began.

One after another, the standard-bearers of Quraysh fell.

The arrows of the archers held the enemy cavalry back, and the Muslim ranks pressed forward.

## Hamzah  (2:29)

Among the bravest was Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the uncle of the Prophet, known as the Lion of Allah.

But hidden among the rocks waited Wahshi, a slave who had been promised his freedom for Hamzah's life.

A single spear was thrown, and the Lion of Allah fell as a martyr.

## Spoils  (2:51)

Soon the lines of Quraysh broke, and they fled, leaving their camp behind.

On the hill, the archers saw the spoils of war in the valley below. The victory is won, some cried.

Abdullah ibn Jubayr reminded them of the Prophet's command. But about forty of them went down.

Only around ten remained.

## Khalid  (3:18)

Khalid ibn al-Walid had been waiting for exactly this moment.

His horsemen rode around the hill, overwhelmed the few archers who remained, and struck the Muslims from behind.

The fleeing Quraysh turned back. In moments, victory turned into chaos.

## Chaos  (3:39)

Then a cry spread across the field: Muhammad has been killed.

Many lost heart. But Anas ibn an-Nadr said: What will you do with life after him? Rise, and die for what he died for.

And he said: I can smell the fragrance of Paradise from beyond Uhud. He charged forward, and was martyred.

## Regroup  (4:05)

In truth, the Prophet was alive, though wounded.

A small band of companions surrounded him, shielding him with their own bodies, among them a woman, Nusaybah bint Ka'b.

Slowly they climbed the slopes of Uhud, and the scattered believers gathered around him once more.

## Abusufyan  (4:27)

From below, Abu Sufyan shouted: Exalted be Hubal!

The Prophet told his companions to answer: Allah is Most High and Most Majestic.

Abu Sufyan called: We have al-Uzza, and you have no Uzza.

They answered: Allah is our Protector, and you have no protector.

Then the army of Quraysh turned, and went home to Makkah.

## Martyrs  (4:56)

Seventy companions were martyred at Uhud.

They were buried where they fell, in the clothes they wore, two and three to a grave.

And the Prophet said: I will be a witness for these on the Day of Resurrection.

## Lesson  (5:15)

Uhud was not the defeat of a faith. It was a lesson, written in sacrifice.

Victory comes with obedience, and the love of this world can turn triumph into loss.

And Allah revealed: Do not weaken, and do not grieve, for you will be superior, if you are true believers.

Surah Al 'Imran, 3:139

## End  (5:44)
